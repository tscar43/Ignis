## API changes

### New header

`X-Fire-Observation-Age` — seconds since the newest satellite observation in
the payload, present when the payload carries a readable observation time.

This is **not** `X-Fire-Cache-Age`. Cache age says when we last fetched;
observation age says how old the data actually is. A cache that keeps
returning a frozen payload reports a cache age of zero forever while its
detections age out. If you surface freshness anywhere in the UI, this is the
number to use — or keep reading `data_as_of.firms` from the payload, which is
the same information.

### Live `/plan` now refuses in four cases

All return 503 with a `detail` string you can show:

1. No fresh evacuation snapshot for the origin. *(existed already)*
2. The fire cache is stale — the refresh loop is behind. *(existed already)*
3. **New:** the newest fire observation is older than 6 h, or its timestamp
   cannot be read.
4. **New:** the configured road graph is outside the bbox the fire model
   covers. Such a graph routes fine and reports *no hazards at all*, because
   the fire polygons are somewhere else entirely — an empty intersection that
   reads as safety. This is a server misconfiguration, not a user error.

`mode=demo` and `mode=replay` are not gated by any of these.

### Dataset failures are now 503, not 500 or 422

A missing or corrupt fixture used to reach you as a **500**, and a fixture that
parsed but failed the contract reached you as a **422** — which told you your
request was malformed when the problem was a file on the server. Both are 503
now, across `/fire`, `/scenario/*`, `/palisades`, `/shelters` and `/plan`.

If you have client code branching on 422 to mean "bad request", it is now
correct to treat 422 as genuinely your fault and 503 as ours.

### `/ready` checks more

It loaded the demo frame and never touched the Camp replay, so it could report
ready while every `/scenario/*` offset returned 503. It now validates each
replay offset it serves. Same 200 / 503 shape.

## The Palisades numbers changed, and so did the claim

`demo_data/palisades_replay.json` and `palisades_comparison.png` were both
regenerated. If `PalisadesView.jsx` reads the fixture, it will now draw
different bars.

The old fixture had Ignis beating the FARSITE-class ellipse on all four
windows. That was an artefact: the ellipse baseline was built once from the
calibration window's wind and reused for every window, so on every held-out
window it was pointing at the wrong weather while Ignis had the right one. The
comparison was measuring the baseline's handicap.

Current growth IoU, all three models:

| window | Ignis | FARSITE-class | FARSITE-class, no fuel/slope |
|---|---|---|---|
| fit, +12.0 h | **0.471** | 0.443 | 0.185 |
| held out, +10.9 h | 0.128 | **0.176** | 0.093 |
| held out, +12.0 h | 0.346 | **0.429** | 0.388 |
| held out, +10.9 h | **0.370** | 0.274 | 0.320 |

**Do not say Ignis beats the industry-standard formulation.** On held-out
windows it is 1–2.

What is supportable, stated carefully — the fuel and slope terms earn their
place but not everywhere. Comparing the two FARSITE-class columns isolates
them exactly, since those two runs differ in nothing else: the terms win three
windows of four, and on the fit window the margin is large (0.443 against
0.185). They lose the last window, 0.274 against 0.320. So: *"the fuel and
slope terms help on most windows, substantially so on some"* — not "always".

Note this cuts against the Camp/Dixie ablation in `validate.py`, where the
fuel term does **not** help. Two fires said one thing, this one mostly says
the other. That is worth being honest about rather than picking whichever
supports the slide.

`demo_data/risk_replay.json` was also regenerated — h6 at T+0 is 204.6 km² against
the old 208.5. Same shape, same contract, nothing to change on your side.

Two other claims were withdrawn and should not go in a pitch:

- The "validated against BehavePlus to a constant 1.029" figure for
  `rothermel.py`. It was measured under a reasoning error and there is no
  reference table checked in to re-measure against.
- The MAGI ensemble spread as a *confidence interval*. It is model
  disagreement. Say "the models disagree beyond here", never "we are 90% sure".
